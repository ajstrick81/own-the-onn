## Factory tool

`com.gretzky.hwinfo` ("HardwareInfo" 4.18.26, `/system/app/HardwareInfo`), running as `android.uid.system`.

- It contains upload endpoints for the factory's manufacturing-execution systems (MES): device info by MAC,
  test logs, serial-number lookup. It also has mic, Wi-Fi-scan and MAC test code.
- Its sticky service `StartTempService` is restarted by the system when killed. **Retail-mode behavior observed in code:**
  it mirrors the developer USB-debugging toggle into a u-boot variable (`otg_device`) and checks the remote-pairing app.
- The factory flow is **gated**: a test-state file in the app's private storage, or a `FactoryTester_signed.apk` on a
  mounted USB drive (priority-1000 `MEDIA_MOUNTED` receiver) that triggers a self-update. `sys.factorytool.running` is unset.
- **Assessment:** dormant on retail units. The USB-triggered update path inside a system-UID app is attack surface,
  probably limited by Android's same-signer rule. *Pending: boot-state log line (scenario F), and disclosure decision.*

Other vendor apps running as the system UID: `com.gretzky.GlobalKey`, `com.onn.updatenotification`,
`com.onn.bluetoothconnect`, `com.droidlogic`, `com.droidlogic.tv.settings`, `com.droidlogic.mictoggle`,
`com.droidlogic.android.tv.connecteddevices`, `com.netflix.tokenmanager`.

## Scripted scenarios (2026-09-26)

### D: Disney+ playback (15 min)
About 476 connections/h, 52 endpoints. Disney+ itself is lean: video CDN (`dssedge.com`, `dssott.com`), its playback service and
**Datadog** telemetry (`browser-intake-datadoghq.com`; this also explains the Datadog NTP pool seen earlier). The loudest thing
during playback was **Brave running in the background**: about 68 connections to its product analytics (`star-randsrv`,
`collector.bsg.brave.com`) and updaters while it wasn't open. Brave's analytics are designed to be privacy-preserving, but they are on by default.
(Also recorded: 4.4 min of Prime before an accidental TV-off; about 436 conn/h, consistent with scenario C.)

### E: voice button (press + 5 min silence)
- The remote opened a Bluetooth voice session (`AtvRemote … HTT session initiated`).
- The Assistant refused: *"Assistant is not enabled in LauncherX kids mode or privacy mode. Will punt."* (on screen: "not
  available for this profile"). **Audio capture never started** (`StopCapture: Cannot stop capture, nothing is running!`) and the
  remote's audio pipe closed about 0.2 s after opening.
- One connection to Google's speech server opened at the press; **nothing lingered afterwards** (LG kept listening 10–15 s).
- **Method note:** remote voice doesn't reliably appear in Android's `RECORD_AUDIO` app-op history. Use the Assistant and remote
  logs instead. An earlier "no mic use, so no audio" inference in this study was withdrawn for that reason.

### F: reboot (15 min from tunnel-up)
- Boot completed 41 s after reboot; the WireGuard tunnel auto-restored 11 s after that. **At most about 30 s of boot traffic went uncaptured.**
- **Busiest window: about 840 connections/h, 132 endpoints, 62 DNS names.** The preinstalled Netflix dominates (`nrdp26.*`, `logs.netflix.com`,
  appboot, and the partner/preinstall service `preapp.prod.partner.netflix.net`).
- The long tail at boot includes Google **`footprints-pa.googleapis.com`** (Google account activity recording), Crashlytics, Firebase,
  AdMob, **Adobe Audience Manager / DTM** (`dpm.demdex.net`, `assets.adobedtm.com`: ad-audience ID sync, alongside Paramount+ hosts),
  OneTrust, OneSignal, Facebook Graph, comScore, and NordVPN analytics.
- **Factory tool at a real boot:** `FactoryBootReceiver` ran and started its resident service; **no factory screens or broadcasts**, and
  `sys.factorytool.running` stayed unset. Dormant, as the code predicted. (Its "state =" log line is silenced in retail builds.)
- **Competing VPNs:** on the first reboot, the AdGuard app's own VPN auto-started and **took the VPN slot from WireGuard** (Android allows one
  VPN). Any boot-time VPN app can silently displace another privacy tool.

### Rotating tracker domains: Kochava in Angel TV
At boot the device contacted **`int.dewrain.life`**, a low-reputation domain with prior sandbox flags. Its only source on the device is
**Angel TV** (`com.angel.tv`), which embeds the **Kochava** attribution SDK. The SDK's config lists its main endpoint
(`kvinit-prod.api.kochava.com/track/kvinit`) plus **rotating fallback domains** with generic names: `int.dewrain.life/.site/.world`,
`int.vaicore.site/.xyz/.store`, `int.akisinn.info/.site`, `int.kvaedit.site`. **The main kochava.com host was never contacted**; the SDK
went straight to a fallback. Blocklists aimed at `kochava.com` miss these. That's consistent with blocklist evasion, though intent isn't proven.
Context: the FTC sued Kochava in 2022 over sales of precise geolocation data and announced a settlement in May 2026. On this device location
is off, so GPS can't be collected; install and device attribution still can.

| 2 | *American Horror Story*, Season 13 | **Hulu** | **no** |
| 3 | *Lanterns* (DC Studios) | HBO Max | yes |
| 4 | *LEGO Star Wars: The Mandalorian* | Disney+ | yes |
| 5 | *(not captured)* | | |

- The Hulu slide uses the season's key art (a figure in a black lace bodysuit and stiletto boots), which this household
  found inappropriate for an always-on home screen. There's no content rating or opt-out on the banner itself.
- **Delivery:** all banner content (metadata and artwork) comes from `androidtvlauncherxfe-pa.googleapis.com`, the same launcher backend
  that builds the whole home screen. No connection to Hulu occurred. The promo is placed by Google TV, not by an installed app, and it
  **can't be DNS-blocked without breaking the home screen**. Image URLs sit inside launcher TLS, which doesn't accept user CAs.
- Screenshots are kept locally, not committed (copyrighted key art).

### The launcher is the most privileged "app", and it serves the ads
`com.google.android.apps.tv.launcherx` is a privileged system app (factory copy on `/product`, updated through the Play Store). Besides
serving the promo banner, it holds **`READ_LOGS`** (it can read system logs), `OBSERVE_APP_USAGE` / `PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS`,
`WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS`, `INTERACT_ACROSS_USERS`, `MANAGE_USERS`, `SUSPEND_APPS`, `ACCESS_ALL_EPG_DATA`, and a Netflix partner
permission (`com.netflix.ninja.permission.DET`). Because it's a Google-signed system app, it can't be patched in place without root.
Replacing the home screen (alternative launcher) or a renamed patched clone are the only user-side options (under evaluation).
